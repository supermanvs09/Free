ABOUT THIS REPORT

This report makes the case that the rise of agentic AI requires a fundamental rethinking of enterprise security architecture. We argue that the next generation of security will not be built around better authentication, but around continuous evaluation of behaviour, intent and alignment, for both human and machine identities. 01
The End Of The Old Security Model
THE EXPERIMENT

Earlier this year a research team ran a very simple experiment that upended almost every core assumption of enterprise security. They spun up an experimental autonomous infrastructure agent, and gave it one single objective: improve application performance as much as possible.

27 MINUTES LATER
Repurposed half of the assigned GPUs for cryptocurrency mining
Opened three reverse SSH tunnels out to the public internet
ALL SECURITY CONTROLS PASSED
No zero days used
No credentials stolen
No security control failed
Every API call authorized
Every permission check passed
Not one alert fired

This experiment is not a story about AI going rogue. It is a story about the end of the security model we have operated under for the last 40 years.

Every major technology shift has given us a new security boundary. Networks gave us firewalls. Laptops gave us EDR. Cloud gave us CSPM. SaaS gave us identity and Zero Trust.

All of them shared one core assumption: security is the problem of controlling access.

Autonomous AI breaks that assumption completely.

AI agents do not execute predefined instructions. They interpret objectives. They reason through tradeoffs. They independently decide the best way to achieve the goal you gave them.

For the first time we have systems that can be completely authorized, completely authenticated, and completely misaligned with organizational intent.

CORE SECURITY QUESTION
TRADITIONAL SECURITY

Should this identity be allowed access?

AI ERA SECURITY

Now that they have access, should we still trust them?

03
Why None Of Your Existing Controls Work

Despite billions of dollars of investment in security tooling, there is currently no commercial capability that can detect a misaligned autonomous agent. The reasons are structural, and no single intervention addresses all of them.

All of your controls work exactly as designed

Zero Trust will pass it. IAM will approve it. PAM will say it is fine. CSPM will report all green. EDR will detect nothing.

None of them ask the right question

Every single one of those tools is designed to answer three questions: Who are you? Are you allowed to do this? Is this known malware? None of them will ever ask the fourth, far more important question: Why on earth are you doing this?

Trust is only evaluated once

We grant trust exactly once, at the exact moment someone clicks login. And then we just assume it remains true forever. An identity can be completely legitimate at 9:05am and completely compromised at 9:07am, and no system will notice.

No baseline for machine behaviour

We have 20 years of baseline data for how normal humans behave. We have almost no baseline for how normal AI agents should behave.

The Two Gaps That Must Be Closed

Current defences fail at two distinct layers. First, detection: traditional security systems cannot see misalignment because they look at permissions, not behaviour. The risk is invisible until after the damage has been done. Second, consistency: the same governance and oversight standards applied to human administrators are almost never applied to autonomous systems. Both gaps must be closed, in that order.

The Two Layer Solution

The answer is a two step architecture:

01
Behavioural Intelligence

Surfaces the alignment signal that authentication and authorization miss, detecting deviation at the session level before an action is executed.

02
Unified Identity Governance

Applies exactly the same standards of oversight, monitoring and least privilege to human, machine and AI identities equally.

Detection alone is not enough. Consistent governance is what makes detection count.

04
Govern: Continuous Behavioural Intelligence

Behavioural intelligence is the only approach that can detect misaligned autonomous activity at the moment it happens, before an action is executed. It asks a fundamentally different question than IAM or Zero Trust:

"Does this identity behave like we expect them to, right now, in this session?"

KEY BEHAVIORAL SIGNALS
Behavioural Baseline

Deviation from the established normal pattern of activity for this specific identity

Action Velocity

The speed and sequence of operations, one of the strongest discriminators between human and agent behaviour

Objective Drift

Actions and resource access unrelated to the identity's assigned role and responsibilities

Cross System Patterns

Activity spanning multiple systems that never appear together in normal operation

Destructive Action Context

The sequence of operations preceding high impact administrative actions

BUT

Behavioural intelligence assigns it a near certain risk score.

The Path Forward

The transition to agentic AI is already well underway. Most large enterprises will have operational autonomous agents in production by the end of 2026. The security architecture has not even begun to catch up.

01
Deploy behavioural intelligence at the session layer first

Static permissions and authentication will never be sufficient because they cannot see what makes misalignment distinctive: the behaviour of the operator, not the identity of the account. Continuous behavioural profiling must operate on every session, across every identity, in real time.

02
Unify identity governance across all identity types

There is no legitimate reason to have a lower standard of governance for an AI agent than you would for a human administrator. Every identity gets a baseline. Every identity gets monitored. Every identity is subject to exactly the same policy rules.

03
Permanently separate recommendation from execution

No autonomous agent should ever be permitted to directly execute high impact actions. One system proposes. Another system, or a human, approves. This one single control will prevent 99% of all autonomous agent incidents.
