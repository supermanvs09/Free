ABOUT THIS REPORT

This report makes the case that tackling India's mule account epidemic requires a two-layer response: first, deploying behavioural intelligence to detect mule activity that KYC and rules cannot see, then feeding those signals into a centralised fraud management system that shares intelligence across institutions and triggers coordinated action. It closes with how Deep Algorithms' adapID Platform delivers exactly this architecture.

01
The Invisible Crime Infrastructure

A mule account is a legitimate bank account co-opted (with or without the owner's knowledge) to receive, layer, and forward illegally obtained funds. The account holder becomes an unwitting or complicit intermediary, providing criminal networks with plausible, KYC-compliant cover for illicit transactions.

What makes them so dangerous is their camouflage. Unlike hacked accounts, a mule account was legitimately opened, belongs to a real person, and generates patterns that appear entirely normal in isolation. It is only when viewed in aggregate, across banks, channels, and time, that the fraud signature becomes visible.

TYPE	HOW CREATED	OWNER AWARENESS	DETECTION CHALLENGE
Fraudulently Opened	Fake or stolen KYC documents	Zero (identity theft victim)	KYC failure; hard to link to perpetrator
Compromised Account	Owner lured, coerced, or purchased into providing access	Partial or full	Indistinguishable on any single-bank view
02
The Scale of India's Crisis

India's combination of rapid digital financial inclusion, a large population with low fraud literacy, and fragmented banking oversight has made it a prime target for mule network operators, many coordinated from overseas.

01

26.5 lakh layer-1 mule accounts identified by I4C as of December 31, 2025. Only ₹8,189 crore of the ₹20,000 crore siphoned has been recovered.

02

Explosive growth: Active mule accounts surged from 80,000 in 2023 to over 1.47 lakh by mid-2025, an 83% increase in under two years, far outpacing detection.

03

Tenfold fraud surge: Cyber fraud cases rose from 2,677 in FY20 to 29,082 in FY24. India's cybercrime portal logged 36 lakh complaints in 2024 alone, with losses of ₹22,845 crore.

04

Economy-level impact: I4C projects annual losses from cyber-enabled fraud could exceed ₹1.2 lakh crore in 2025, roughly 0.7% of India's GDP.

"

Around 99% of frauds won't happen if mule accounts do not exist. It's very surprising to see how easily such accounts are opened and comfortably used by cybercriminals.

03
How Mule Networks Operate

Mule accounts are not standalone crimes. They are nodes in a coordinated layering infrastructure designed to make illicit funds untraceable before they reach their final destination.

01
🎣
The Crime

A victim is defrauded via phishing, fake investment schemes, digital arrest scams, or account takeover. Funds leave the victim's account instantly.

02
🏦
Mule Entry

Stolen funds land in a Layer-1 mule account, typically within minutes. Each account is used briefly, then abandoned.

03
🔀
Rapid Layering

Funds are split across multiple mule accounts at different banks. By the time any one institution flags a transaction, money has already moved two or three hops further.

04
💸
Exit

Laundered money exits via ATM cash withdrawal, cryptocurrency, hawala networks, or overseas transfers, often through Southeast Asia-based operators.

CASE STUDY: CHHATTISGARH-CAMBODIA SYNDICATE

Police uncovered a network whose leaders operated call centres in Cambodia, targeting Indians via romance and job scams. Over ₹10 crore in two years was layered through 30-50 mule accounts, then moved overseas via cryptocurrency and hawala. The accused had systematically purchased Indian bank accounts from associates in a single Chhattisgarh district, with accounts selling for ₹5,000 to ₹3.5 lakh each.

04
Why Current Defences Are Falling Short

Despite significant regulatory activity, the mule account problem is growing, not shrinking. The reasons are structural, and no single intervention addresses all of them.

🔎
Siloed Detection

Each bank monitors its own transactions in isolation. A mule chain spanning three or four institutions is invisible to any single participant.

📋
Rule-Based Systems

Eight of ten banks studied still rely on basic threshold monitoring. Fraudsters fragment transactions specifically to stay below these thresholds.

⏱️
Detection Lag

Traditional monitoring flags suspicious accounts days or weeks after the money has moved. By then, the mule account has been abandoned.

📱
Channel Blind Spots

Fraud flows simultaneously across internet banking, mobile banking, UPI, and core banking. Single-channel monitors miss the cross-channel pattern.

🆔
KYC as a Weak Gate

Aadhaar-based KYC allows a single individual to theoretically hold credentials across dozens of accounts, each passing KYC in isolation.

🌐
Cross-Border Coordination

Criminal operators based in Southeast Asia make takedown operations complex. By the time legal coordination completes, the network has reorganised.

THE TWO GAPS THAT MUST BE CLOSED

Current defences fail at two distinct layers. First, detection: rule-based systems cannot see mule behaviour because they look at transaction amounts, not behavioural signals. The fraud is invisible until after the money has moved. Second, coordination: even when one bank flags a mule account, that intelligence stays siloed; other institutions continue processing transactions from the same network, unaware. Both gaps must be closed, in that order.

THE TWO-LAYER SOLUTION

The answer is a two-step architecture: behavioural intelligence surfaces the mule signal that rules miss, detecting fraud at the session level before money moves. Then a centralised fraud management system shares those signals across institutions and triggers coordinated action network-wide. Detection alone is not enough. Coordination is what makes detection count.

05
Detect: Behavioural Intelligence

Behavioural intelligence is the only approach that can detect mule activity at the moment it happens, before the money moves. It asks a fundamentally different question than KYC or rule engines:

"Does this account behave like a mule, right now, in this session?"
⌨️
Keystroke and Navigation Patterns

Mule operators managing multiple accounts exhibit scripted, repetitive interaction patterns. Login speed and navigation sequence differ measurably from genuine account holders.

📱
Device Reuse Across Accounts

The single most powerful mule signal: one device authenticating to 10, 20, or 30+ accounts. Each passes KYC individually, but device intelligence sees the pattern.

📍
Geo and Time-of-Day Anomalies

Sessions from locations or times inconsistent with the account holder's established profile, especially when paired with immediate outbound transfers.

🔗
Cross-Channel Layering Signatures

Mule activity spans channels: a fund receipt via NEFT followed within seconds by a UPI transfer. Cross-channel correlation identifies this layering pattern.

📲
SIM-Swap and App Cloning

Device fingerprinting detects tampered devices, SIM-swaps, and cloned mobile banking apps, common tools used to convert genuine accounts into mule vehicles.

⚡
Velocity and Session Anomalies

Legitimate holders rarely log in at 2 AM, transact within three seconds, and log out immediately after transfer. Velocity and session duration are reliable discriminators.

WHY BEHAVIOUR BEATS RULES: A CONCRETE EXAMPLE

A ₹4,999 transfer stays below any rule threshold and triggers no alert. But if that transfer occurred 2.8 seconds after login, from a device that authenticated to 24 other accounts today, at 1:47 AM, from a location 800 km from the registered city, behavioural intelligence assigns it a near-certain mule risk score. The rule engine saw nothing. The behavioural engine saw everything that mattered.

The Path Forward

India's mule account crisis is large, fast-growing, and structurally enabled by a banking infrastructure not designed to detect coordinated, cross-institutional fraud at network speed. The regulatory response represents genuine progress, but implementation remains uneven and the tools are still maturing.

01
Deploy behavioural intelligence at the session layer first

Static KYC and transaction thresholds will never be sufficient because they cannot see what makes mule accounts distinctive: the behaviour of the operator, not the identity of the account. Continuous behavioural profiling must operate on every session, across every channel, in real time.

02
Then centralise and share that intelligence

A detection made in isolation protects one bank. A detection propagated in real time to every institution in the ecosystem protects all of them simultaneously. Cross-bank signal sharing and coordinated adaptive actions convert individual detections into network-wide coverage.

03
Build for explainability and compliance
