BACKGROUND

Think of Mythos-class models as AI systems that can reason through complex tasks the way a highly skilled expert would, except they work at machine speed and do not need sleep, do not make typos, and do not lose patience.

These autonomous AI models can read a system, identify its weak points, write code to exploit those weak points, and cover their tracks, all in one session. They can do this without any prior knowledge of the specific system they are targeting. They learn as they go.

For most industries, this is a concern. For banking, it is a structural crisis. Banking runs on trust in transactions. Every time you tap your card, transfer money, or receive a salary, a chain of invisible systems confirms that the money is real, the account is valid, the transfer is authorized, and the record is permanent. That chain has been built and patched over decades, and it works because attackers needed time, expertise, and physical effort to break it.

Mythos-class models do not need time. They do not need expertise they were not given. And they require no physical effort at all.
01
RISK 01
HIGH
Banks Have No Defense Against an Autonomous AI Cyberattack That Learns Their Systems in Minutes

Before stealing anything, an attacker needs to understand the bank they are targeting. Every bank's internal network is different. The way funds flow, the way staff communicate, the way transactions are authorized varies institution by institution.

This was the natural defense. Understanding a specific bank took months.

The Carbanak gang, which stole close to one billion dollars from over 100 financial institutions, spent two to four months inside each bank's network before moving a single dollar. They infected computers through phishing emails, then quietly recorded staff screens to learn how the transfer systems worked. Only once they understood the bank's internal behavior did they act.

THE DETECTION WINDOW
The entire banking security industry, its monitoring systems, its alert thresholds, its human analysts, is built around attacks that unfold over days and weeks. An attack that completes in under an hour is invisible to it. Mythos-class AI models have demonstrated the ability to autonomously complete 22 out of 32 steps of a full corporate network attack simulation with no human guidance. The protective window that gave defenders time to respond has collapsed.
02
RISK 02
HIGH
The $850 Million That Was Saved by a Spelling Mistake, and Why AI-Powered SWIFT Fraud Will Not Make That Error

In the Bangladesh Bank SWIFT fraud, an incident that shook the global financial system, hackers sent 35 fraudulent transfer instructions through SWIFT, the global messaging network used by nearly 11,000 banks worldwide to move money internationally. They had already stolen Bangladesh Bank's legitimate SWIFT credentials, built malware that deleted transaction logs, and blocked confirmation messages from printing.

Five instructions went through successfully. $101 million was transferred. The remaining $850 million was blocked because the attackers misspelled "Foundation" as "Fandation." Deutsche Bank flagged the error. The transfer was stopped.

That typo was not a security feature anyone designed. It was luck.

WHAT CHANGES WITH MYTHOS-CLASS AI
A Mythos-class model generates perfectly formatted SWIFT transfer messages. It does not make spelling errors. It operates around the clock, meaning it does not wait for a weekend when fewer staff are monitoring systems. Every accidental defense that stopped that attack, the typo, the timing, the manual printer checks, becomes irrelevant. The same attack, run by an autonomous AI model, transfers all 35 instructions perfectly.
03
RISK 03
HIGH
Your ATM Balance Can Say One Thing While Your Money Is Already Gone

When you withdraw cash, your bank records a debit: your balance goes down. But banks also have a rollback function, a legitimate tool used to reverse transactions that were processed incorrectly. It is a routine, necessary feature of any financial system.

A criminal group called Metel once gained access to a Russian bank's internal money-processing system and automated this rollback function maliciously. Every time a withdrawal was made at an ATM, the debit was instantly reversed in the bank's ledger. The balance appeared unchanged. The cash was gone.

The group drove teams of people across multiple Russian cities, visiting ATMs through the night and draining cassette after cassette. They needed cars, people, and physical presence to execute it.

A Mythos-class AI removes every physical requirement. An attack that required a coordinated criminal operation across multiple cities becomes a software task completed from a single machine.
04
RISK 04
CRITICAL
One Flaw in One Vendor. Thousands of Banks Compromised by an AI-Powered Exploit at the Same Moment.

Most people assume that hacking a bank means targeting that bank specifically. In reality, the financial sector runs on shared infrastructure. The majority of the world's banks use a small number of third-party vendors for file transfers, payment processing, and data management.

This creates a situation where a single vulnerability in one piece of software is simultaneously a vulnerability in every institution running that software.

In May 2023, a ransomware group called CL0P exploited a flaw in MOVEit, a widely used file transfer tool. The result was a cascade that compromised over 2,700 organizations. CISA estimated the final count at over 3,000 US organizations and 8,000 worldwide. The financial damage reached approximately $9.93 billion.

THE AI ACCELERATION PROBLEM
CL0P had been manually testing this vulnerability for nearly two years before deploying it. A Mythos-class autonomous AI model does not need two years. It compresses the entire discovery and testing phase into a single session. One model. One shared vendor. One prompt. The preparation that took a human group years of quiet work becomes an afternoon task. Every bank using that vendor is exposed in the same moment.
05
RISK 05
CRITICAL
Transaction Finality, the Rule All of Banking Is Built On, Can Be Broken by an AI Exploit

Transaction finality is the principle that once a payment is settled, it is permanent. You cannot unsend money that has cleared. This is not just a feature. It is the foundation that makes banking function at all. Without it, no transaction can be trusted after the fact.

The systems that enforce this are called Hardware Security Modules, or HSMs. These are physical devices that cryptographically sign and verify transactions, ensuring that each one is unique, authorized, and tamper-proof. They are the last line of defense against someone rewriting transaction history.

The Bangladesh Bank attack gave a glimpse of what happens when transaction records are compromised. But that attack still required human experts who knew exactly which system to target.

HSM VULNERABILITY AND AI
Mythos-class AI models have already demonstrated the ability to autonomously find vulnerabilities in systems that survived decades of human security review, including a 27-year-old flaw in OpenBSD. Many banking HSMs are older than OpenBSD. An AI that can generate a second, cryptographically valid version of a transaction with the same reference number but a different destination creates a situation where no one can determine which version is real. Transaction finality does not break loudly. It breaks quietly.
06
RISK 06
HIGH
Security Patches Take 90 Days. AI-Powered Exploits in Banking Take Minutes.

There is always a gap between when a security flaw is found and when it is fixed. The banking industry works on a 90-day standard patching window, and in heavily regulated financial environments the timeline is often longer, because every patch must be tested, approved, and deployed without disrupting live transaction systems.

This gap was considered acceptable because finding a working exploit was hard. It took skilled humans months or years. CL0P tested the MOVEit vulnerability for nearly two years before weaponizing it. Even then, patches arrived just four days after the first exploitation, but the damage was already done.

THE GAP KEEPS GROWING
Mythos-class autonomous AI models achieve an 83% first-attempt success rate in finding working exploits. The discovery-to-exploit timeline, previously measured in months, is now measured in minutes. The 90-day patch window has not gotten shorter, it cannot, because regulation requires testing. But the attacker's side of the equation has already collapsed. By the time a patch is developed for one vulnerability, an AI model has already found the next one.
07
RISK 07
HIGH
Every Bank Employee Becomes a Target, Simultaneously and Personally, Through AI-Generated Spear Phishing

The technical attacks described above all share an entry point: a human being who was tricked. Carbanak began with spear-phishing emails sent to bank employees. The Bangladesh Bank breach began with a keystroke logger installed on bank computers. The GCMAN group maintained silent access to a bank's network for 18 months, eventually transferring $200 per minute through a scheduler that was never logged internally.

Every one of these attacks required human criminals to craft individual targeting strategies, write specific emails, and wait patiently for someone to make a mistake.

A Mythos-class model can generate individually tailored social engineering messages for every employee at every bank at the same time. Not template spam. One specifically crafted message per person, calibrated to that employee's role, writing style, known colleagues, and daily patterns.

The attack that Carbanak spent months preparing for a handful of targets becomes a single batch job covering an entire sector simultaneously.

3 RISKS THAT HAVE NOT HAPPENED YET, BUT WILL

The seven risks above are grounded in attacks that already occurred. These scenarios follow directly from what Mythos-class AI models can do.

MICRO-TRANSACTION BLEEDING

Fraud detection systems are trained to flag large, unusual transactions. They are not designed to catch thousands of transfers just below the detection threshold happening simultaneously. An autonomous AI model that understands how a bank's fraud engine works can probe it silently, learn its thresholds, and drain accounts one small transaction at a time across thousands of accounts in a pattern that looks like normal activity.

FRAUD MODEL POISONING

Banks use machine learning models to detect fraudulent transactions. An attacker with AI capabilities can deliberately feed incorrect data into those models over time, slowly training the bank's own fraud detection to ignore a specific type of attack. By the time the real attack begins, the defense has been quietly disabled from the inside.

PEAK-LOAD TIMING

Banking systems process the highest volume of transactions at specific predictable times: end-of-month payroll runs, tax deadlines, holiday retail periods. An AI model can identify these windows and time an AI-powered financial attack to coincide with peak processing load, where anomalies are hardest to detect and human monitoring is most stretched.

WHAT THIS ALL COMES DOWN TO

Every major banking attack in the last decade succeeded because attackers had enough capability to get in and not enough capability to avoid every mistake. A typo stopped $850 million. Years of preparation gave defenders time to react eventually. Physical presence at ATMs limited how fast cash could be removed.

Mythos-class AI models remove the capability ceiling without adding any of the human limitations.

The attacks that defined financial cybersecurity for the past decade were not worst-case scenarios. They were what happened when skilled human attackers did almost everything right. The question banks now have to answer is what happens when the attacker has no ceiling on capability and no floor on the mistakes they are allowed to make.

That question does not have a comfortable answer yet. But it is being asked right now.
